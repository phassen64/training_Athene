6.2 Inference Optimization Techniques

Large Language Models (LLMs) require significant computational resources during inference (the process of generating predictions or responses after training). As organizations deploy LLMs in cloud environments, optimizing inference becomes essential to reduce latency, improve throughput, lower infrastructure costs, and enhance user experience.

Inference optimization techniques help organizations serve more users efficiently while maintaining acceptable model accuracy. Three of the most widely used optimization techniques are Quantization, Batching, and Caching. Let's explore these in below section.

Quantization:

Quantization is the process of reducing the numerical precision of model weights and activations. Instead of using high-precision data types such as 32-bit floating-point (FP32), the model uses lower-precision formats such as FP16, INT8, or INT4. The goal is to lower the model's size and improve its computing efficiency without sacrificing too much performance.

How It Works?

In deep learning models, parameters (weights and activations) are typically stored using 32-bit floating-point (FP32) numbers. While highly accurate, FP32 values consume significant memory and computational resources.

Quantization converts these values into lower-precision formats such as 8-bit integers (INT8), reducing storage requirements and accelerating computations.


Figure 6.5: Quantization Examples

As shown in the diagram, floating-point numbers are mapped to integer representations while preserving their approximate numerical meaning. This allows AI models to perform inference more efficiently with minimal impact on accuracy. By replacing 32-bit values with 8-bit values, the model requires less storage, transfers less data between memory and processors, and executes calculations faster.

Top LLM Quantization Methods:

Quantization techniques reduce the precision of model weights and activations, enabling Large Language Models (LLMs) to use less memory, run faster, and consume less power while maintaining acceptable accuracy. Quantization techniques can be classified based on when and how the model parameters are converted to lower precision.




Figure 6.6: Top LLM Quantization Methods

The diagram presents the two primary approaches used to quantize Large Language Models (LLMs): Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) and Post-Training Quantization (PTQ).

Quantization-Aware Training (QAT): Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) is a technique that simulates low-precision computations during training to produce a highly accurate quantized model.

Key Characteristics:

* Performs quantization during training or fine-tuning.

* Requires the full training dataset.

* Simulates low-precision operations (e.g., INT8 or INT4) during training.

* Produces highly accurate quantized models with minimal performance loss.

* Requires additional training time and computational resources.

* Best suited for accuracy-critical production applications.

Post-Training Quantization (PTQ): Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) is a technique that converts a fully trained model to lower precision without retraining, using a small calibration dataset.

Key Characteristics

* Applied after model training is complete.

* Requires only a small calibration dataset.

* No retraining or fine-tuning is required.

* Simple, fast, and cost-effective to implement.

* May introduce slight accuracy degradation, especially at lower bit widths.

* Ideal for rapid deployment, edge devices, and resource-constrained environments.

Batching:

Batching is an inference optimization technique that processes multiple input requests together as a single batch instead of handling each request individually. By grouping inputs and executing them simultaneously, batching improves hardware utilization, increases throughput, and reduces the overall cost of AI inference.

In cloud-based AI systems and Large Language Models (LLMs), batching is commonly used to maximize the efficiency of GPUs and AI accelerators, which perform parallel computations more effectively when processing multiple inputs at once.




Figure 6.8: Batching Technique

The diagram illustrates the concept of batching, where multiple user requests are collected and processed together by a GPU as a single batch rather than handling each request individually.

* Inputs from various sources, such as chat messages, API calls, documents, and application requests, are grouped into a batch and sent to the GPU for parallel processing.

* The GPU efficiently executes computations on all requests simultaneously and generates multiple outputs in a single inference cycle.

* By leveraging the parallel processing capabilities of GPUs, batching significantly improves hardware utilization, increases throughput, and reduces the cost per inference.

* Instead of allocating resources separately for each request, the system shares computational resources across multiple requests, enabling scalable and cost-effective AI deployments.

Types of Batching Strategies:

Static Batching: Groups a fixed number of requests into a batch before processing, providing high throughput for predictable workloads.

Key Characteristics:

* Requests are collected into fixed-size batches before execution.

* The system waits until the predefined batch size is reached.

* Provides high throughput and efficient GPU utilization but may increase latency if requests arrive slowly.


Dynamic Batching: Collects requests during a short time window and processes them together, adapting the batch size based on incoming traffic.

Key Characteristics:

* Incoming requests are collected over a short configurable time window.

* The number of requests in each batch varies depending on traffic.

* Offers a balance between latency and throughput by adapting to changing workloads.



Continuous Batching: Continuously adds new requests to an already running batch, maximizing GPU utilization and minimizing latency.

Key Characteristics:

* Requests are continuously added to the processing queue while earlier requests are still being executed.

* Eliminates the need to wait for a complete batch, keeping the GPU continuously occupied.

* Delivers the lowest latency and highest hardware utilization, making it ideal for real-time LLM serving.

Caching:

Caching is a performance optimization technique that stores previously computed data so it can be reused instead of being recomputed. In LLM applications, caching reduces latency, lowers computational costs, improves throughput, and enhances the user experience by avoiding redundant processing.




Figure 6.11: Caching Workflow

The diagram illustrates the workflow of an LLM caching mechanism, where previously generated responses are stored and reused to minimize redundant model inference.

1. Step 1: User Query and Prompt

* The user submits a query or prompt to the LLM application.

* The application receives the request and prepares it for processing.

2. Step 2: Check Cache

* Before invoking the LLM, the application checks the LLM cache (e.g., Redis or an in-memory cache).

* The cache is searched for a previously stored response that matches the incoming prompt.

3. Step 3: Return Cached Response (Cache Hit)

* If a matching response exists in the cache (a cache hit), the application retrieves and returns it directly to the user.

* Since no model inference is performed, the response is delivered much faster while consuming minimal computational resources.

4. Step 4: Invoke the LLM (Cache Miss)

* If the requested response is not found in the cache (a cache miss), the application forwards the prompt to the Large Language Model (shown as PaLM 2 in the diagram).

* The LLM generates a new response.

* The generated response is then stored in the cache for future requests before being returned to the user.

Cost Governance for GenAI Workloads:

Cost governance is the practice of planning, monitoring, controlling, and optimizing the operational costs associated with deploying and managing GenAI applications. It involves tracking cloud resource usage, allocating costs across teams, setting budgets, and implementing optimization techniques that reduce unnecessary spending without compromising application performance.

Cloud cost governance operates at a different level. It focuses on control and intent. It answers questions like:

* Who is allowed to create spend?

* Under what constraints?

* How are those constraints enforced as systems change?



Figure 6.17: Cloud Cost Governance Framework

Cloud Cost Governance Lifecycle:

* Measure (Track Usage and Spend): Continuously monitor cloud resource consumption, service utilization, and operational costs. Collect usage metrics and spending data to identify trends, anomalies, and cost drivers.

* Allocate (Assign Ownership): Assign cloud resources and associated costs to specific teams, projects, or business units. Clear ownership improves accountability and enables accurate budgeting and chargeback/showback practices.

* Optimize (Improve Efficiency): Analyze resource utilization and eliminate inefficiencies through rightsizing, auto-scaling, workload scheduling, storage optimization, and the removal of idle or unused resources.

* Review and Adjust (Refine and Evolve): Regularly evaluate cloud spending, governance policies, and optimization strategies. Incorporate operational feedback and changing business requirements to continuously improve cost efficiency and resource management.

As generative AI continues to transform digital education, language learning platforms are leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) to provide personalized, interactive, and adaptive learning experiences. By deploying LLM-powered features on scalable cloud infrastructure, educational platforms can deliver real-time conversational practice, customized explanations, and intelligent feedback to millions of learners worldwide. This cloud-native approach enables high availability, rapid model updates, and seamless integration of AI capabilities into existing learning workflows.

* Organization: Duolingo

* Target Industry: Education Technology (EdTech)

Problem Statement:

Duolingo serves millions of learners with varying proficiency levels, learning goals, and native languages. Traditional rule-based exercises and predefined responses were effective for basic language instruction but lacked the ability to provide natural conversations, personalized explanations, and context-aware feedback. Delivering AI-powered language tutoring at a global scale also required significant computational resources, high availability, and the flexibility to continuously improve AI models while maintaining low response times.

Proposed Solution:

Duolingo adopted a cloud-based LLM architecture to power advanced AI learning experiences. Using cloud-hosted Large Language Models integrated with its learning platform, the company introduced intelligent features such as AI-driven conversations, personalized lesson explanations, contextual grammar assistance, and adaptive feedback. The cloud deployment enables elastic scaling to support millions of concurrent users, centralized model management, secure API integration, and continuous deployment of updated AI models without disrupting learners.

Outcome

* Delivered highly personalized language learning experiences tailored to individual learners.

* Improved conversational practice through realistic AI-generated dialogues.

* Increased learner engagement and retention with interactive AI tutoring.

* Enabled rapid deployment of new AI capabilities using cloud infrastructure.

* Achieved scalable and reliable AI services capable of supporting a global user base.

* Reduced operational complexity through centralized cloud-based model management.

Key Takeaways

* Cloud deployment provides the scalability required for LLM-powered educational platforms.

* Large Language Models significantly enhance personalized and adaptive learning experiences.

* Cloud-native AI enables continuous model improvements without affecting end users.

* Intelligent conversational AI improves learner engagement and practical language skills.

* Integrating LLMs with cloud infrastructure accelerates innovation while ensuring reliable service delivery.

* Personalized AI tutoring demonstrates how cloud-hosted generative AI can transform digital education at global scale.

Case Study 2:

Organizations today depend on digital workspaces to manage projects, collaborate across teams, and maintain organizational knowledge. However, employees often spend considerable time creating documents, summarizing meetings, searching for information, and switching between multiple applications. This case study explores how Notion AI addresses these challenges by integrating Generative AI into its workspace platform, enabling intelligent content creation, knowledge retrieval, and workflow automation to enhance overall productivity and collaboration.

Title: Notion AI's Integration for Workspace Productivity

Background:

As organizations increasingly rely on digital workspaces for collaboration and knowledge management, employees often spend significant time searching for information, documenting meetings, drafting content, and organizing projects. To address these productivity challenges, Notion integrated Generative AI directly into its all-in-one workspace platform. By combining document management, project tracking, databases, and AI-powered assistance, Notion AI enables users to generate content, summarize documents, answer questions from workspace knowledge, and automate repetitive writing tasks within a single collaborative environment. This integration helps teams work more efficiently while reducing the need to switch between multiple productivity applications.

* Organization: Notion Labs Inc.

* Target	Industry:	Productivity	Software,	Enterprise	Collaboration,	Knowledge Management, and Project Management

Problem Statement:

Organizations faced several workspace productivity challenges, including:

* Time-consuming document creation and editing.

* Difficulty locating relevant information across large knowledge bases.

* Manual meeting note summarization and task extraction.

* Frequent context switching between writing tools, search applications, and project management software.

* Reduced collaboration efficiency due to scattered documentation.

Proposed Solution:

Notion integrated Generative AI capabilities directly into its workspace platform by:

* Embedding AI-powered writing assistance for drafting, rewriting, and editing content.

* Automatically summarizing lengthy documents and meeting notes.

* Providing intelligent question-answering using workspace knowledge.

* Generating action items, project plans, and task lists from meeting discussions.

* Supporting brainstorming, content generation, translation, and document refinement.

* Leveraging retrieval-based AI to access relevant organizational knowledge securely within the workspace.

Outcome:

* Improved employee productivity by reducing time spent on documentation and content creation.

* Faster retrieval of organizational knowledge through AI-powered search and Q&A.

* Enhanced collaboration with automated meeting summaries and task generation.

* Reduced context switching by integrating AI directly into the workspace.

* Increased consistency and quality of documentation across teams.

* Streamlined project management and knowledge-sharing processes.

Key Takeaways:

* Integrating Generative AI into productivity platforms significantly improves workplace efficiency.

* AI-assisted writing and summarization reduce repetitive manual work.

* Knowledge-aware AI enables faster decision-making by surfacing relevant information.

* Embedding AI within existing workflows minimizes disruption and increases user adoption.

* Secure AI integration with enterprise knowledge bases enhances collaboration while maintaining organizational context.
