    url:https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_der_Dinge
    Das Internet der Dinge bezeichnet die Verknüpfung eindeutig identifizierbarer physischer Objekte (englisch things) 
        mit einer 
    virtuellen Repräsentation in einer Internet-ähnlichen Struktur. 
    Es besteht nicht mehr nur aus menschlichen Teilnehmern, 
        sondern auch aus Dingen. 
    Der Begriff „Internet of Things“ geht auf Kevin Ashton zurück, 
        der ihn 1999 erstmals verwendete. 
    Jedoch ist dies nicht schriftlich dokumentiert.[6] 
    Bekannt wurde das Internet der Dinge durch die Aktivitäten 
        der „Auto-ID Labs“.[7]

u:  a55 IoT Devices—Common Features
    
    +   Local Control
        Example: 
            Switches on the device enable user to turn it on or off directly and 
        to start Wi-Fi configuration routine.

    +   Remote Control
        Example: 
            Switches power on or off through remote app 
                or “smart home” voice-controlled speakers.

    +   Connected
        Example: 
            Connects to cloud through Wi-Fi to send sensor data and 
                device status and 
                receive commands from apps.

    +   Smart
        Example: 
            Onboard microcontroller monitors sensors, 
                controls switching, 
                and communicates with cloud servers.

    +   Local Outputs
        Example: 
            LED on the device changes color to show on/off status and 
            when it enters configuration mode.


    +   Remote Sensing
        Example: 
            Sensors in the device monitor power usage and 
            conditions such as overheating and overcurrent.

u:  c08 Data Analytics
    ~ The science of examining raw data to derive insights and 
        conclusions that help businesses make better decisions or 
        to verify or disprove existing models or theories.

    Subset of required mathematical skills.
    Four main types:
        •   Descriptive
        •   Diagnostic
        •   Predictive
        •   Prescriptive{"verordnend,empfehlend"}

u:  c09 "Descriptive Analytics"
 
    ~   A form of data analytics that uses data 
        to summarize past behaviors. 
                
    Any time in the past.
    
    Answers the question :: 
        "What has happened?"
    
    Mathematical operations like summation and average.
        Total stock in inventory
        Averages sales by department
        Sales growth month over month
    Use descriptive analytics when:
        You need a high-level understanding of what's going on in your company.
        You want to summarize and describe different aspects of your business.


u:  c10 Types of Descriptive Analytics
    Business intelligence (BI)
    Data mining
    Data visualization


u:  c11 "Diagnostic Analytics"
    ~ A form of data analytics that focuses on 
            examining past data to identify the causes of specific events.

u:  c12 Types of Diagnostic Analytics
    Identification of reasons for changes in market demand.
    Explanation of customer behavior.
    Identification of technology issues

u:  c13 "Predictive Analytics"
    
    ~   A form of data analytics that uses data to 
        forecast future behaviors. Estimation of future outcomes.
   
    Answers the question :: 
        "What could happen in the future?"
    Commonly used in forecasting.

u:  c14 Types of Predictive Analytics
    Predictive modeling
    Root-cause analysis
    Data mining
    Pattern identification and alerts

u:  c15 Prescriptive Analytics
    
    ~   A form of data analytics that uses data to 
            predict multiple possible outcomes and 
            then offers recommendations for actions based 
        on those predictions.
    
    A step beyond predictive analytics.
    Answers the question ::
        "What should we do?"
    Uses business rules, algorithms, ML, and computational modeling.
    Data inputs:
        Historical data
        Transactional data
        Real-time data
        Big data
        
u:  c16 Types of Prescriptive Analytics
    Simulation
    Optimization

=== s:  Types of Data

u:  c23 "Big Data"
    
    ~   Collections of data that are so large and

        analyze them.

    •   Velocity

u:  c24 Structured, Unstructured, and Semi-Structured Data

    a)  Structured
        Contains elements that are easily accessible for 
        effective analysis
    
    b)  Unstructured   
        Is not organized in an established manner and 
        does not conform to a data model.

    c)  Semi-Structured Data
        Data stored outside a relational database that has 
        characteristics that support analysis.

u:  c25 Open and Proprietary Data
    
    Open data: 
        Freely available for use.
        Can be shared or built on by anyone for any reason.
        Should be legally and technically open.
    
    Proprietary data: 
        Owned/controlled by an individual, organization, or group. 
        Protected by copyright, patent, or trade-secret legislation

=== s:  The Data Science Lifecycle

u:  c30 Lifecycle Stages
    
    1.  Problem Identification
    2.  Data Collection 
    3.  Preprocessing 
    4.  Exporatory Data Analysis
    5.  Modeling
    6.  Model Deployment 
    7.  Communication Results => 1.
    
u:  c31 Problem Identification   
    Roles:
        Stakeholders
        Data-team manager
        Data scientist

u:  c32 Data Collection 
    Roles: 
        Data scientist
        Data analyst
        Data-team manager
        Database administrator

u:  c33 Preprocessing
    Improves data quality to facilitate analysis.
    Tasks:
        Data wrangling/munging
        Data cleaning
        ETL

u:  c34 Exploratory Data Analysis
    Summarizes main characteristics of datasets.
    Visual representation of summary information.
    Can use different plot types.
    Can identify when additional preprocessing is needed.

u:  c35 Modeling 
    Develops an ML model to solve the problem.
    ML models: algorithms and model data.
    Feature engineering.
    Model training and evaluation.
    Model validation.

u:  c36 Model Deployment
    Integrates the ML model into existing systems.
    Operationalizing:
        Moving into production.
        Integration via APIs/executables.
        Results: repeatable, scalable, and flawless.
    Monitoring:
        Outside influences affect performance.
        Periodically verify the model generates expected results. 
        When thresholds are exceeded, 
            revisit earlier stages to adjust the model.

u:  c37 Communication of Results
    Gather results and communicate them to stakeholders.
    Identify key findings documented throughout the process stages.
    Create visual representations of insights.
    Package it all into reports for stakeholders.

=== s:  Improve Customer Experience

u:  d04 "Customer Experience" (CX)
    
    ~   The overall perception of a company based on 
        customers' interactions with  that company.
    
    Based on subjective responses.
    Covers all stages of the customer journey.
    Highly individualized in details but improving CX benefits the business.
    Potential use cases:
    •   Personalization
    •   Sentiment analysis
    •   Recommender systems
    •   Self-service support
    •   Chatbots
    •   Virtual assistants
    
u:  d05 Personalized CX
    Helps customers feel valued.
    Identify services and interactions that lead to positive reactions.
    Collect data at all stages of the customer journey.
    Initial enquiry->Comparison->Purchase->Implementation & Use -> Loyality

u:  d06 "Sentiment Analysis"

    ~   A process that determines the emotional tone of textual data, 
        often grouping results in 
            positive, negative, and neutral categories.

    Common sources:
        Emails
        Customer-support chat transcripts
        Online reviews
        Social media comments
    Technologies used: ML, statistics, and NLP.
    Insight into customers' mindsets.
    Uncover possible ways to increase customer satisfaction.

u:  d07 "Recommender Systems"
    
    ~ A form of ML that suggests potential items of 
        interest based on a customer's profile, search history, 
    and inventory metadata.

    Past interest becomes a factor to predict future interest.
    
    Examples:
        Books by same author
        Books in same genre
        Companion products
        Clothing in similar styles or colors

u:  d08 Self-Service Support
    Get help when you need it.
    Optimal for resolving basic issues.
    Implementation options:
        Chatbots
        Knowledgebases/documentation
        Online forums/communities
    Combine with traditional customer support.
    How does data science help?

u:  d09 Chatbots and Virtual Assistants

    Generative AI: 
        Creates content that appears convincingly humanlike.
        Text, audio, images, and video

    Chatbots
        Simulate human conversation via text or voice messages.
        On websites, in apps, and on messaging platforms.
        Use a conversational interface.
        Primary goal: 
            to provide customer support.

    Virtual assistants
        Assist users with performing simple tasks.
        Commonly built into devices.
        No interface needed.
        Primary goal: 
            to help users conduct business or personal activities.

$>  AI ROAS =: Return on Ad Spend
        refers to the use of artificial intelligence and

$>  AI KPIs (Key Performance Indicators)

u:  d15 Marketing Measurement Analysis
    The process of collecting, 
        analyzing, and interpreting data to measure 
        the effectiveness of marketing activities.
    A step beyond campaign optimization.
    Analyzing effectiveness of prior activities helps drive success 
        of future campaigns.

    Metric & Description
    Reach:      
        Number of people who are exposed to a marketing message.
    Frequency:  
        Number of instances a person is exposed to a 
                    marketing message.
    Engagement: 
        Level of interaction with a marketing message.
    Conversion:
