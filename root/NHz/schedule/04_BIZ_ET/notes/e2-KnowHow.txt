$   Internet of Things (IoT)
    <  ~ refers to a  network of physical objects ("things") 
    embedded with sensors, 
        processing power, software, 
    and connectivity technologies to collect and 
        exchange data with other devices and systems over 
    the internet or local networks.


<   Core Architecture (4 Pillars) : {"Säule"}
    
    Perception Layer (Sensing): 
        Physical hardware equipped with sensors 
            (temperature, motion, pressure) and 
    actuators (motors, relays) that interact with the environment.

    Connectivity Layer (Networking): 
        Communication protocols 
        that transmit gathered data to local nodes or 
        the cloud (e.g., MQTT, CoAP, Wi-Fi, 
        Bluetooth LE, LoRaWAN, NB-IoT, Cellular).

    Data Processing Layer (Middleware / Cloud / Edge): 
        Processing raw telemetry data, 
        performing real-time analytics, filtering, 
        and running machine learning inference 
        (often using Edge Computing or Cloud IoT hubs like AWS IoT Core, 
        Azure IoT Hub, or GCP Cloud IoT).

    Application Layer: 
        Front-end dashboards, user interfaces, or 
        automated feedback loops 
        (e.g., smart home controllers, 
        industrial SCADA integrations, predictive maintenance triggers).


$   IoT Examples
    Smart Thermostats (e.g., Ecobee, Google Nest):
        Sensors: Temperature, humidity, ambient light, 
        occupancy (PIR).
        Connectivity: Wi-Fi, Matter/Thread.
        Function: 
            Learns home usage patterns, 
            adjusts HVAC systems dynamically to save energy, 
            and allows remote control via smartphone apps.   


$   Safety vs Security 

    Obwohl Safety und Security im Deutschen häufig beide 
        mit „Sicherheit“ übersetzt werden, 
    bezeichnen sie im Ingenieurwesen, 
        in der IT und im Risikomanagement 
        zwei grundlegend unterschiedliche Konzepte.

    Der Kernunterschied lässt sich kurz zusammenfassen:

    Safety (Funktionale / Arbeitssicherheit): 
        Schutz der Umwelt und Menschen vor den Gefahren 
        eines Systems 
        (Schutz vor <unbeabsichtigten> Fehlern, Unfällen 
            und Naturereignissen).

    Security (Informations- / Objekt-Sicherheit): 
        Schutz des Systems vor den Gefahren aus der 
        Umwelt 
        (Schutz vor <vorsätzlichen>, bösartigen Angriffen 
            und Manipulationen).

$   clustering model

    clustering model helps you perform targeted marketing primarily 
        by segmenting customers into 
        distinct, 
    homogeneous groups based on shared behaviors, 
        purchasing patterns, 
    or demographics, allowing you to tailor campaigns to each group.

    Key examples of how this works in practice include:

    Customer Segmentation: 
        Grouping customers by purchasing 
            history (e.g., high-value frequent buyers vs. bargain hunters) 
            so you can send personalized product recommendations or 
            targeted discount offers.

    Churn Risk Identification: 
        Grouping users by engagement frequency and feature usage 
            to identify declining usage patterns early and 
            send targeted re-engagement campaigns.

    Customized Communication Channels: 
        Clustering customers based on communication preferences or 
        response rates so you reach each segment 
        via their preferred channel (email, SMS, push notification).

    Behavior-Based Persona 
        Creation: Identifying natural clusters i
        n raw behavioral data 
        (e.g., late-night impulse shoppers vs. weekend planners) 
        to design distinct messaging for each customer persona.

    Core Principle: 
        Clustering is an >unsupervised< learning technique 
        that discovers hidden patterns 
        and natural groupings in data without 
        predefined labels, making it ideal for discovering unexpected market segments.

$   IoT gateway

        program that serves as the central connection hub between 
        field IoT devices (sensors, actuators, smart appliances) 
    and the cloud or data center.
    It acts as a network bridge and central translator, 
        enabling low-power local sensors to communicate 
        securely with remote cloud platforms.

    Lightweight Protocols :Zigbee, BLE, Modbus, LoRaWAN             
    Standard Protocol : 
        HTTP/MQTT over TCP    HTTPS, MQTT over TCP, REST
             
$   IoT device    
    An IoT device (Internet of Things device) is a 
        non-standard computing hardware object—such as a 
    sensor, smart appliance, wearable, or industrial machine—that 
        connects wirelessly to a network and transmits or receives data.
    Unlike traditional computing endpoints 
        (like laptops or smartphones), 
    IoT devices operate autonomously in physical environments 
        to monitor, automate, and interact with real-world conditions.

    Question 25:  	Which of the following cybersecurity concerns are generally more applicable to IoT devices than to client computers, cloud services, and IoT gateways? (Choose three.) 	
 	 	 
 	 	 
Option 1: 	
	Because they need to conserve energy, they may have minimal encryption capability.
 	 	 
Option 2: 	
	Because they often rely on wireless connections, they may be subject to communication jamming.
 	 	 
Option 3: 	
	Because they store large amounts of data, they are valuable targets.
 	 	 
Option 4: 	
	Because they are often mobile or located in remote locations, it may be challenging to provide them with physical protection from attackers.
 	 	 
Option 5: 	
	Because they process personal data, they are subject to strict data-protection regulations.

Die drei richtigen Antworten sind:
• Option 1: Because they need to conserve energy, they may have minimal encryption capability.
• Option 2: Because they often rely on wireless connections, they may be subject to communication jamming.
• Option 4: Because they are often mobile or located in remote locations, it may be challenging to provide them with physical protection from attackers.


Erklärung:

Diese drei Sicherheitsrisiken treffen spezifisch und in weitaus höherem Maße auf IoT-Endgeräte (wie eingebettete Sensoren oder Aktoren) zu als auf klassische PCs, Gateways oder Cloud-Dienste:

1. Ressourcenbeschränkung (Option 1): 
Viele IoT-Geräte sind extrem klein und müssen 
mit minimaler Energie (z. B. jahrelang über eine Batterie) 
auskommen. Daher fehlt ihnen oft die Rechenleistung für komplexe, asymmetrische Verschlüsselungsverfahren, die auf PCs oder in der Cloud Standard sind.

2. Drahtlose Kommunikation & Jamming (Option 2): 
IoT-Geräte kommunizieren überwiegend über drahtlose Protokolle (wie Zigbee, Bluetooth, LoRaWAN). 
Dadurch sind sie anfällig für Signalstörungen (Jamming) oder das Abfangen von Funksignalen aus der Ferne.
3. Physische Erreichbarkeit (Option 4): Während Server in geschützten Rechenzentren stehen und PCs meist in überwachten Büros genutzt werden, sind IoT-Sensoren oft im öffentlichen Raum, an Außenwänden, in Fahrzeugen oder an abgelegenen Orten (z. B. in der Landwirtschaft) installiert. Angreifer können daher leicht physischen Zugriff erlangen, um die Hardware zu manipulieren oder auszulesen.
Warum die anderen Optionen nicht primär auf IoT-Geräte zutreffen:
• Option 3 ist falsch: IoT-Endgeräte erfassen zwar kontinuierlich Daten, speichern selbst aber meist nur minimale Mengen und leiten sie direkt weiter. Große Datenmengen akkumulieren sich stattdessen in der Cloud oder auf zentralen Datenbanken.
• Option 5 ist falsch: Die Einhaltung von Datenschutzrichtlinien (wie der DSGVO) gilt für alle Systeme, die personenbezogene Daten verarbeiten. Cloud-Dienste und Server-Infrastrukturen tragen hierbei aufgrund der zentralen Datenspeicherung meist die ungleich größere regulatorische Last.
