$>  BigData
    Big Data refers to datasets so 
        large, complex, and fast-growing 
    that traditional data-processing software cannot 
        capture, store, or analyze them within a reasonable timeframe. 
    Rather than a single technology, 
        it represents an ecosystem of modern data architectures, 
        distributed systems, and modern analytics practices.
    The Core Characteristics (The "5 Vs")
    •   Volume
        Massive quantities of data measured in 
        petabytes or exabytes (e.g., social media activity, 
        transaction logs, device sensors).
    •   Velocity
        The rapid speed at which data is generated and must be processed in 
        real-time or near-real-time.
    •   Variety
        Diverse formats ranging from 
            structured (relational databases)   to 
            semi-structured (JSON, XML)         and 
            unstructured (video, audio, text).
    •   Veracity:
        The accuracy, quality, and trustworthiness of 
                messy, noisy raw data.    
    •   Value
            The ultimate goal of turning raw data into actionable insights 
                for decision-making.

$<  Aufbau eines neuronalen Netzwerks
    Ein typisches Netzwerk ist in 
        mehreren Schichten (Layers) organisiert:
    Eingabeschicht (Input Layer): 
        Nimmt die Rohdaten auf (z. B. Pixel eines Bildes, Zahlenwerte oder Wörter).
    Verborgene Schichten (Hidden Layers): 
        Hier findet die eigentliche Datenverarbeitung statt. Bei Netzwerken mit vielen verborgenen Schichten spricht man von Deep Learning.
    Ausgabeschicht (Output Layer): 
        Liefert das Endergebnis (z. B. die Entscheidung: 
        „Das Bild zeigt eine Katze“).

$>  In-Context Prompting 
    ~   is a technique where you provide examples, 
        background context, or explicit formatting guidelines directly 
        within the prompt to guide 
        a large language model's (LLM) output 
        without updating its underlying weights (fine-tuning).
    Instead of retraining the model, 
    you leverage its short-term "in-context learning" ability 
        through the input text.

    +   Zero-Shot Prompting
        Concept:    Direct instruction without prior examples.
        Example:    "Translate 'hello' to French."

    +   Few-Shot Prompting
        Concept: Providing one or more target 
            input-output pairs to demonstrate the desired structure, 
            tone, or format before giving the actual query.
    
        Example:
            Input: Happy    -> Output: Positive
            Input: Terrible -> Output: Negative
            Input: Alright  -> Output: Neutral
            Input: Fantastic -> Output:  Positive

$>  LLM Text Completion 
    ~ is the foundational mechanism behind Large Language Models (LLMs). 
    At its core, an LLM operates as an autoregressive token predictor: 
        given a sequence of input text (the prompt), 
        the model evaluates statistical patterns learned 
    during training to calculate probability distributions and predict 
    the single most likely next token. 
    It appends that token to the context and repeats the 
    process until a stopping criterion is met.

$>  Reasoning Model (RLM)
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reasoning_model
    
    A ~ is an AI system designed to spend additional 
        computational power during inference (at response time) to 
            think,
            plan, and 
            evaluate 
        intermediate steps 
    before outputting a final answer.   
    
    While standard Large Language Models (LLMs) generate 
        immediate responses token-by-token in a single forward pass, 
    reasoning models generate a 
        private or internal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trace. 
    This allows them to explore multiple solution strategies, 
        check intermediate logic, 
        backtrack from dead ends, 
        and correct their own errors prior to showing the answer.

$>  Retrieval-Augmented{de.zunehmen} Generation (RAG)        
    ~ is an architectural pattern in AI that enhances 
        Large Language Model (LLM) responses by 
    dynamically fetching relevant context from 
    external authoritative knowledge bases before generating an answer.
    Instead of relying solely on static facts learned during pre-training, 
        RAG grounds the model's output in up-to-date, 
            proprietary, 
            or domain-specific data, 
    drastically reducing hallucinations and eliminating the 
    need for constant model fine-tuning.

$>  Negative Prompting    

$<  Negative prompting is essentially 
        the opposite of how prompts thus far have 
        been shown—
    instead of instructing the model what to create, 
        it instructs the model what not to create. 
    This enables you to constrain the image output more 
        because the negative prompt helps to remove or
        reduce the presence of unwanted elements. 
    Some models are more sensitive to negative prompting than others.

$<  Ein Negative Prompt (negativer Prompt) ist eine 
    Anweisung an ein KI-Bildgenerierungsmodell, 
    die festlegt, welche Elemente, Stile oder Fehler im generierten Bild 
        <vermieden> oder 
        <ausgeschlossen> 
    werden sollen.
    Während der normale (positive) Prompt beschreibt, 
        <was> im Bild zu sehen sein soll 
        (z. B. „Ein magischer Wald mit leuchtenden Pflanzen“), 
        steuert der Negative Prompt gezielt das, was 
        <ausgeblendet> oder 
        <korrigiert> 
    werden soll.

$>  Frame interpolation
    Frame interpolation (also known as motion-interpolated temporal 
        upsampling) 
    is a video processing technique that generates 
    intermediate frames between existing frames 
        to increase the frame rate (FPS).
    It converts lower frame rate media (e.g., 24 FPS or 30 FPS) 
    into smoother, 
    higher frame rate video (e.g., 60 FPS, 120 FPS, or higher), 
    creating fluid motion and enabling slow-motion effects.

$>  Fine Tuning Image and Video Models
    Fine-tuning image and video models adapts pre-trained 
        diffusion models (e.g., FLUX, Wan 2.1, HunyuanVideo, Mochi 1) 
        or vision-language models to learn specific characters, 
    artistic styles, camera movements, or domain-specific features.
    Rather than training a multi-billion parameter model from scratch, 
    fine-tuning injects new knowledge using a small, 
    specialized dataset (often 10–100 images or 5–20 short video clips).

$>  AI A/B text testing        
    AI text generation A/B testing generally refers 
        to two distinct practices depending on your objective:
    1.  Testing AI-Generated Text vs. Human Text 
        (Marketing & Product Copy): 
            Using Generative AI to produce text variations 
            (headlines, subject lines, ad copy, CTAs) 
            and running A/B tests against human-written 
            copy or baseline messaging to optimize conversion rates.

        (AI Engineering & Product Development):

        prompts, or configurations 
        (e.g., GPT-4o vs. Claude 3.5 Sonnet, or Prompt V1 vs. Prompt V2) 
        to see which produces better user engagement, accuracy, or conversion.

    Group A (Control / Variant A): 
        Receives the human-generated response.
    Group B (Treatment / Variant B): 
        Receives the AI-generated response.

$>  In-Context Code Debugging 
    ~ is an AI technique where a Large Language Model (LLM) detects, 
    explains, and fixes bugs in software by processing the 
    problematic code, environment logs, 
    and instructions directly inside its prompt context—without 
    updating or retraining its underlying model parameters.   
    Instead of relying on weight adjustments (like fine-tuning), 
    the model leverages its In-Context Learning (ICL) 
    ability to perform 
    static code analysis, trace execution paths, 
    and synthesize fixes on the fly.
