u:  a08 General AI and Narrow AI
    
    General AI
        Same or better cognition than humans
        Extrapolates reasoning
        Solves multiple novel{"neu,ungewöhnlich"} problems
        Not yet a reality

    Narrow AI
        Approximates human cognitive traits
        No true capacity for reason
        Solves one or a few designated problems
        Currently a reality

        Unlike human intelligence,

        Every AI deployment in real-world use today—including 
        Advanced Large Language Models—falls under the category of Narrow AI.

u:  a21 Generative AI

    Generative AI: 
        Deep learning models used to generate any 
            kind of content that appears as if it were created by a human.

    Trained on massive amounts of data.
        Disparate sources and types.

    You can "ask" a model to create by:
        Providing a written prompt.
        Providing media to use as an example.
        Providing media for transformation.
        Receiving suggestions based on work.
        Any combination of these.

u:  a26 Prompt Engineering

    Prompt engineering: 
        The process of refining an input prompt until it 
        produces a desired output from a generative AI model.

    Necessary skill for anyone using generative AI.
    No universal "good" prompt.
    General best practices:
        Build prompts iteratively.
        Be descriptive.
        Prefer clarity to verbosity.
        Provide relevant examples.
        Save and reuse successful prompts.

        Complaint: The product was broken when it arrived. Reason: Defect.
        Complaint: I ordered the red version but got the blue one instead.

u:  a28 Fine Tuning

    Fine tuning: 
        The process by which an existing model's weights are 
            updated based on new training data.


    Augments model outputs.
    Customizes model for specialized domains.
    Reduces need for in-context prompting.
    Requires data, time, and resources.

=== s:  Identify:<Shortcomings>         of Generative AI

u:  f04 Confabulation/Hallucination
    Confabulation{"Plauderei"}: 
        The phenomenon in which a model 
        produces an output that appears false, 
        incoherent, or unjustifiable by the data it was trained on...
    +   Also called hallucination (misnomer).
    +   LLM confabulation:
        Direct factual errors.
        Indirect factual errors.
        Continuing from false premises.
        Misinterpretation of user input.
    +   Reasons for confabulation not fully known.


u:  f05 Misinformation and Misleading Content
    +   LLMs can spread misinformation.
    +   Some chatbots reject inappropriate requests.
        Restrictions can still be circumvented.
    +   Fake audiovisual content also a problem.
    +   Not all misleading content is generated intentionally.
        Due to misleading/outdated training data.
        For models: garbage in, garbage out.

u:  f06     Limitations of Training Data (Slide 1 of 2)

    +   Outdated knowledge.
        "Frozen" in time.
        Output could be overtly wrong.
        Or, subtly wrong.

    +   Lack of scope and diversity.
        Example: Mangled hands in image generation.
        Training images don't feature many clear shots of hands.
        Hands are complex.
        Model fails to learn how to consistently draw hands.


u:  f08 Other Quality Issues    
    +   Chatbots tend to respond in a rote, 
        dry way.
        Limiting when creativity is desired.
        May be due to restrictions placed by model operators
    +   Can create a false sense of authoritativeness.
        Due to randomness.
        Same prompt sent to same model may give different 
              results at different times.
        A "good" result may be a fluke.

u:  f09 Vibe Coding
    Vibe coding: 
        The practice of working with an AI code-generation tool 
            in a quick and careless way, 
        without truly understanding the code it produces.
    +   Benefits for developing low-impact code.
    +   Many drawbacks for critical production code.
            ->  App will be flawed and cause disruption.
    +   Don't rely on vibe coding.
    +   Use AI as an assistant, not replacement.
            ->  Review, validate, and sanitize generated code.

u:  f10 Cost of Implementation
    Basic costs for third-party web apps.
        Subscriptions per user.
    Costs with using APIs.
        Price per call adds up.
        Example: chatbot uses up many tokens per day.
    Costs with self-hosted infrastructure.
        Bandwidth and processing needs.
        May need new server infrastructure.
        Development, maintenance, and support costs.

u:  f11 Hardware Requirements
    Minimal requirements if you rely solely on cloud services.
    Typical software development requirements for using APIs.
        Special considerations for storage.
    Considerable requirements if you host your own services.
        Large GPU clusters.
        High network bandwidth.
    Most orgs will rely on cloud services.

u:  f12 Training Time and Output Time
    Training a model can take weeks or months.
        On high-end hardware.
    Fine tuning can also take time depending on data.
    Outputs also take time.
        Might be waiting seconds, minutes, or hours.
    Time-sensitive apps can encounter problems.
    Smaller models produce faster outputs.

u:  f13 Lack of Fine-Tuned Control
    Not all models support fine tuning.
    May need to rely on a model you can't customize.
        Acceptable to some organizations.
    Fine tuning isn't always feasible.
        Requires adequate data.
    Fine tuning doesn't guarantee good results.
        Model might overfit to new data.

u:  f14 Reliance on Foundational Models
    Centralization of generative AI capabilities.
    Based on a few core models.
    Flaws can proliferate downstream.
        Become accepted/ignored.
    May not be suitable for specialized purposes.
    May become out of date.

u:  f15 Reliance on Cloud Services
    No control over hosting infrastructure.
    API can change at any time.
    No control over outages or restoration efforts.
    Issues with networking and required connections.

u:  f27 Governance
    Governance{"Regierungsführung}: 
        The act of putting various mechanisms, 
            processes, and structures in place to direct and

    Ensures issues are addressed:

u:  f28 Impact on Employees
    Generative/agentic AI can displace jobs.
        Mostly jobs that produce intellectual content.
        Artists, writers, programmers, etc.
    Employees may feel "technostress."
        Lost productivity.
        Job insecurity.
    Employees may struggle to adapt.
        AI can disrupt workflow.
        Reduced productivity during transitional period.

u:  f29 Future of Work
    Generative/agentic AI will change how people work.
        Includes how and who.
    Implications:
        Shifting tasks
        New job roles
        New skill requirements
        Creative collaboration
        Analytical mindset
        Lifelong learning

u:  f30 Operational Risks
    Disruptive AI brings operational risks.
    AI solution may:
        Outright fail.
        Not bring business value.
        Not be cost beneficial.
        Not be well integrated.
        Not be easily updated/maintained.
    Change management can:
        Increase organizational readiness.
        Minimize risks.
        Better prepare org to harness generative AI.

u:  f31 Data Risks
    Generative AI is heavily data driven.
    Applies to training data and output data.
    Areas of concern:
        Data quality
        Data control
        Data security

u:  f32 Brand Reputation and Consumer Trust
    People might see generative AI as:
        A poor-quality replacement.
        Devaluing human work.
    Example: Generated imagery in an ad.
        Something off about it.
        Looks cheap and amateurish.
    Example: Customer-service chatbot.
        Helpful, but people are still leery.
        Accusations of greed and putting people out of jobs.
    Ethical concerns also impact consumer trust.

u:  f33 Intellectual Property and Copyright
    Hotly contested topic.
    Example: Lawsuit filed against Stability AI and Midjourney.
        Images used to train models without permission.
        Claims generated images are derivative works.
    Opponents argue litigants misunderstand the tech.
        Model checkpoints don't store images.
        Claim outputs are suitably transformative.
    Who really owns the output?
    The model owner/operator?
        The user?
        No one?

u:  f34 Legal and Regulatory Considerations
    Traditional legal frameworks are inadequate.
    Technology outpaces the law.
    Recent regulations:
        EU AI Act
        AI legislation for specific U.S. states
        Council of Europe's AI Convention
    Speculative examples:
        Law imposes restrictions on training data.
        Law compels disclosure of AI content/systems to users.
